# Hackathon brief

**Planning draft · [Issue #1](https://github.com/1N4B0M9/NovaHacks2026/issues/1)**

## Problem statement

At closing time, an immigrant restaurant owner who keeps kitchen notes in one language and buys from a supplier whose catalog uses another must translate ingredient names, interpret shorthand, and reconcile quantities with the packs the supplier actually sells. A clear request for two dozen eggs can become a purchase of 180 if someone silently rounds it to a case. The owner needs to see that difference in their preferred language before committing money.

This is a working hypothesis about a specific ordering situation. Immigrant identity alone tells us neither a person's preferred language nor how they order. The merchant chooses the review language.

## Product promise

Write the list the way you already do. See what the supplier can fulfill, check the quantities and cost, and approve the order yourself.

## Evidence and constraints

The NOVA opening slides give a six-hour build window, a five-minute presentation, and a DoorDash Best Merchant Experience track. They invite happy and sad paths and permit prototype elements. The team has two collaborators with separate Codex sessions. App credentials and availability of sample orders are unverified.

## Validation before committing scope

Ask a willing operator to walk through their last reorder with identifying details removed. Which words changed between their list and the catalog? Which units did they check? Have them interpret one review screen and say exactly what the supplier would receive. Ask which two languages the team can validate with a fluent reader. If a merchant is unavailable, mark the fixtures as synthetic and the problem as unvalidated.
